# EUframes_person_full.csv – codebook

A **fully synthetic** person-level companion to the EU-frames case: 41,660
simulated respondents spread across the real 270 country-year cells, carrying
every individual variable the original author's published regressions use. No
Eurobarometer respondent appears in it, and no record in it is derived from any
one real person.

Generated by `companion/_model-outputs/simulate_person_joint.R`. The
[simulation module](../companion/simulation.qmd) explains the construction and
[the individual-level page](../companion/individual-level.qmd) puts it to work.

## What makes it usable

Not that it was built carefully. That the same model was fitted to the real
respondents and to this file, and the two coefficient vectors compared
line by line. That comparison is committed as
`companion/_model-outputs/twin_validation.csv`: 60 of 64 coefficients agree
within two standard errors of the difference, and 46 within one.

The comparison is against the **two-sample** standard error, which asks whether
a difference exceeds this file's own sampling noise. Judged instead against the
precision that 416,698 real respondents afford, 39 of the 64 agree. This file
holds a tenth as many people and cannot stand in at full precision; it can
stand in at its own.

## How it is built

Two stages, answering two questions.

**Who is in the sample.** A respondent's characteristics are one draw from a
latent multivariate normal whose correlation matrix was estimated on the real
respondents. Each observed variable is a monotone transform of one coordinate:
sex and employment status by a threshold, urbanisation by cutpoints, age,
education and left-right through their quantile functions. Every marginal is
reproduced by construction and so is every pairwise association – age and being
unemployed correlate at −0.24, age and education at −0.14, and a generator that
drew them independently would return the wrong coefficients however carefully
each variable's own effect was set.

Occupation beyond the unemployed indicator comes from a multinomial model
conditional on the rest.

**What they say.** Each of the thirteen framing items has a latent propensity
that is linear in the respondent's characteristics, shifted by their
country-year, and carries a residual correlated with the other twelve – a
multivariate probit. Simulating the survey instrument rather than the four
dimension scores is what gives the scores their exact zeros and ones and makes
them sum to one automatically.

**The country-year shift is solved, not set.** A dimension score is a share of
a respondent's own mentions, so raising one dimension's items lowers every other
share. For each of the 270 cells the generator solves four shifts by Newton
iteration against a fixed reference sample until the expected dimension means
are the panel's own, converging to under 1e-4.

## Estimated, calibrated, authored

**Estimated from the public panel** (`EUframes_cy.csv`, committed): every cell's
four dimension means, which are the solve's targets.

**Calibrated from the licensed person-level file**, read once and locally by
`calibrate_joint.R`, `calibrate_items.R` and `fit_person_anchor.R`: 171
characteristic correlations, the marginal distributions, 195 item probit
coefficients, 78 residual correlations, the conditional occupation model, and
the prevalence gradient. All are aggregate statistics of the kind a published
appendix carries, and all are committed under `companion/_model-outputs/`. The
licensed extract itself is never committed.

**Authored** – three constants corresponding to nothing measured, none of them
an effect on what a respondent answers. Every individual-level effect in the
file is estimated. The three govern who enters the sample and how many: the
cell size as a fraction of the real respondent count (0.10), the fieldwork
selection tilt that gives `w1` something to correct (0.15 per decade below the
mean age), and the switch that makes that tilt vary with a cell's unemployment
rate (0 by default).

## Variables

| Variable | Description |
|---|---|
| `pid` | Simulated person identifier |
| `cntry` | ISO 3166 two-letter code, as in `EUframes_cy.csv` |
| `year` | Year (2004–2013) |
| `w1` | Selection weight, the reciprocal of the fieldwork tilt, mean 1 within country-year. **Not a survey weight** – see the caution below |
| `age` | Simulated age in years, from the calibrated quantile grid |
| `female` | Simulated indicator at the calibrated marginal |
| `edu` | Age at which full-time education finished, the form the published band cut is made on |
| `edu3` | Education band: `low` (< 16), `mid` (16–19), `high` (> 19) |
| `ses` | Occupational position, eight categories as published: `manual`, `self_employed`, `manager`, `white_collar`, `house_person`, `unemployed`, `retired`, `student` |
| `unemployed` | Indicator, equal to `ses == "unemployed"` |
| `urban` | `rural`, `small_town`, `large_town` |
| `rile` | Left–right self-placement, 1–10. **Missing for four of the sixteen waves**, mirroring the real data, so about 40% of rows are NA |
| `k` | Number of framing items the respondent mentioned (0–13) |
| `cosmo`, `util`, `comm`, `lib` | Simulated dimension shares; sum to 1 for anyone who mentions anything, to 0 otherwise |
| `pos`, `neg` | Composites: `pos` = `cosmo` + `util`, `neg` = `comm` + `lib` |
| `unemp`, `growth`, `bailout` | Real published values, joined from `EUframes_cy.csv` |

## What it will not support

**Anything at the survey-wave level.** The real file has sixteen waves across
ten years; this one is built on the 270 country-years of the public panel and
its finest cluster is the country-year.

**The interacted form of the published model.** The validation deliberately
uses the specification with its cross-level interactions dropped. A synthesis
built to reproduce a covariance structure does not reproduce a cross-level
interaction unless it is put in, and this one was not.

**Cell-level unemployment slopes are amplified by about a sixth.** The
generator gives each country-year four numbers, one shift per framing
dimension, where the real data has thirteen, one rate per item. A
dimension-level shift captures as little as 37% of some items' cell-to-cell
variation, and what is lost shows up two ways: the mentioning rate runs about
1.6 points below the panel's, and a model fitted to the twin's *cell means*
returns unemployment slopes larger in magnitude than the panel's by roughly
15%. No single draw reveals this - one draw's sampling noise is the same size
as the bias - and it took a sweep of independent draws to separate it from
zero. The individual-level validation above is unaffected, because it compares
person-level coefficients rather than cell-level ones.

Giving each cell its own thirteen item rates was tried and made things worse:
the marginals become exact and the coefficient comparison falls from 39
agreeing to 32, because a cell's real item rates reflect its context and its
mix of people together, and the twin draws its people from pooled marginals.
Repairing it properly would mean reproducing each cell's demographic
composition as well.

**Analyses of individual framing items.** The file reproduces each item's
average salience but not its country-by-country variation, because cell-level
dimension means come from the public panel. No consequence for any model using
the four dimension scores.

**`w1` is not a survey weight.** It reverses a deliberate over-sampling of
younger respondents that the generator introduces so that weighting has
something to correct. It is a teaching device with a known answer, not
Eurobarometer's design weight, and it should not be read as one.

## Reproducing it

```
Rscript companion/_model-outputs/simulate_person_joint.R
```

from the project root. The generator reads only the public panel and the
committed calibration files, so anyone can run it. `$SIM_SEED` selects the
draw, `$SIM_OUT` the destination, and `$SIM_SWEEP` appends one row per outcome
to a file for repeated-draw studies.

On the GESIS terms the position is a reading rather than a settled fact, and it
is worth stating as one. The licensed respondent file was read locally to
estimate the correlations, marginals and coefficients listed above. Those are
aggregate statistics of the kind the usage regulations describe as "summarizing
representations of the data typical to scientific works and presentations", so
releasing them is provided for. The regulations are then silent on synthetic
derivatives: no clause addresses whether a generated file containing none of
the original records falls inside or outside "the provided data". Read on the
ordinary meaning of that phrase, it falls outside, which is why this file is
released under CC BY. Releasing fully synthetic data derived from restricted
microdata is long-established practice – the US Census Bureau's SIPP Synthetic
Beta works this way. Anyone redistributing this file should carry this
paragraph with it rather than the conclusion alone.
